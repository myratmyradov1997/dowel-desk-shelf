# Assembly

Rod positions are numbered **front to back: 1 – 2 – 3 – 4 – 5**. Rods 1 and 5 carry the pinch clamps
(M3 screw in each leg), the MagSafe mount (rod 1) and the rod clips (rod 5).

## 1. Sort and prepare the rods

Store-bought dowels are rarely what the label says. Ours: three were 20.0–20.1 mm, two were 19.0 mm,
lengths differed by ~1 cm and all were slightly bent.

1. **Measure** each rod with calipers in 3–4 places.
2. **Best rods to positions 1, 3, 5.** The clamp (2.4 mm slot) only closes down to ≈ 19.6 mm,
   so a 19.0 rod cannot be clamped at all. **Thin rods go to 2 and 4**, where there is no clamp.
   The deck lies on rods 1/3/5; a thin rod is 0.5 mm lower and just carries less.
3. **Find the bow:** roll each rod on a flat floor, it stops with the bow up. Mark it and assemble **bow down**
   (bow sideways tilts the legs and the drawer channel).
4. **Sand bumps** on the part where an inner leg slides (200–330 mm from the ends). Rub with paraffin.
5. **Same length:** ideally cut all rods to the shortest one (fine saw + mitre box, chamfer the ends).
   Otherwise centre each rod so it enters both end legs equally.
6. **Number the ends** in pencil (1L/1R … 5L/5R) so they go back to the same place.

## 2. Frame

1. Slide the rods through both **inner legs**. Do not tighten the clamps yet (M3 × 20 + nut, nut in the hex pocket).
2. Push the rod ends into the **end legs** (blind sockets, 14 mm). Thin rod too loose? Wrap 4–5 turns of
   electrical tape or use adhesive heat-shrink until it goes in with force. Glue only if you never want to take it apart
   (epoxy or contact glue; PVA does not hold on ABS).
3. Put the **bay gauge** (194 mm) on the desk between the end leg and the inner leg, touching both — front, then back.
4. **Insert the drawer first, then tighten** the two M3 clamps of the inner leg. The gauge lies on the desk but the
   channel is 80 mm higher: a slightly leaning leg is invisible on the gauge but makes the drawer rub.
5. Check the full travel (145 mm). Bay narrower than ~0.5 mm → the drawer rubs on the retainer;
   wider than ~1 mm → it sags and has more play. Repeat on the other side.
6. Mark the inner-leg position on rods 1 and 5 for next time.

## 3. Drawers

- The drawer hangs on its side flanges in the 45° V-channels of the legs.
- **Paraffin on the 45° faces** — ABS on ABS squeaks and wears, especially with 0.28 mm layers. No oil (collects dust).
- **Stop:** a spring tongue with a nub on the end-leg side wall stops the drawer at 145 mm.
  The nub stops at the edge of the window in the leg; to remove the drawer, press the nub inward through that window and pull.

## 4. Deck

- Put the three tiles on the rods, joined by the dovetails. The tile saddles are D21 on purpose, so a tile **sits loosely** —
  it works with any rod 19.8–21 and with bent rods. Monitor weight, the dovetails and the rod clips keep it in place.
- Snap the two **rod clips** onto rod 5 at the tile ends (x ≈ ±271…281 mm); their hooks also hold cables.
- Rattling? Felt or a layer of tape in the saddles.
- Cable notches (8/12/16 mm) in the back edge let monitor cables drop behind rod 5. The shelf can stand against a wall.

## 5. Finishing

- Rubber/EVA strip 1–2 mm under the legs: four legs on one frame plus a bit of ABS warping = wobble;
  the strip also stops the shelf from moving when you pull a drawer.
- If the shelf sways sideways under the monitor: two thin steel cables crossed at the back of the centre span.
- In winter (dry air) the wood shrinks ~0.2 mm — re-tighten the clamps.
